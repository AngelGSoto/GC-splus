Referee report(s) on this version:


Report by referee 1:

This paper is a study of the metallicity of the globular clusters (GCs) in NGC5128 out to unprecedented galactocentric distances. This measurement relies on the combination of broad-band photometry from the literature with new narrow-band observations that allow to better constrain the metallicity in the spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting. The main result of this study is the identification of a metal rich and metal poor population of GCs, both showing radial metallicity gradients, plus an intermediate population with flat gradient. This is interpreted as likely originating from accretion.

This study contains interesting results from novel data that offer valuable additional constraints to accretion history of this galaxy. However the manuscript would benefit from some restructuring, as well as from a more in-depth analysis of the data, and a more rigorous treatment and quantification of the uncertainties. Therefore I recommend this paper for publication after major revisions.

Major comments *

The text needs some restructuring. In principle I appreciate the intention to move part of the details of the analysis to the appendix and leave the main text lighter and more focused on the results, but in a few instances I found that this was not a good idea. This is because i) some parts relegated to the appendix were crucial to understand the analysis - bouncing back and forth between main text and appendix made the reading much harder than necessary - and because ii) leaving out from the main text important details on the choices made or the tests done detracts from the actual work that has been done. I’ll try to advise below on some adjustments.
Since the accuracy of the measurement of the metallicities relies on the narrow band filters, an important information would be the depth reached by the new narrow-band photometry and how this affects the completeness of the final sample of GCs. Line 162 states that there are GCs that might not be detected in some images. This translates in less accurate measurements for some objects or even quality cuts later on in the analysis that likely bias the final sample towards the brighter population. This should be quantified and discussed.
Metallicities from SED fitting are inherently uncertain and model dependent. This aspect is not really addressed in the paper but it does deserve some attention. I would encourage, for example, some exploration of how variable are the results when changing the ingredients in the modelling. There is some mention in the appendix (lines from 1143) of some tests with the BC03 libraries, but there is no mention of whether these new metallicities are still consistent within the uncertainties or whether the gradients persist. I would also try different star formation histories, for example a bursty SFH. All this should be in the main text. Then a clear statement on the magnitude of the uncertainties and how they are derived is important. Is the comparison with spectroscopy similarly satisfying with different modelling choices? Are the metallicity gradients robust against the details of the modelling?
I found the test mentioned in Sect 4.1.2 and then described in the appendix F unconvincing. There the results from the SED fitting w/ and w/o narrow-band photometry are compared with metallicity from spectroscopy. Since the comparison is somewhat worse with broad-band photometry only, the conclusion is that narrow-bands help in constraining better the metallicity. The main issue with this is that the broad-band only test is carried on a much larger sample of 123 GCs vs 94 from the comparison before, and it is not even clear how much overlap there is between the two samples. If the point of the test is just to show that the metallicity posterior distribution from the SED fitting is much narrower and better constrained with the use of narrow-band filters, then why not show exactly that? In the comparison, only the same objects should be used.
The quantification of the metallicity gradients needs to take into account for the uncertainties on the metallicities. Is the fitting method weighting by the uncertainties? To test the robustness of the errors on the slope, I suggest to use some statistical test based on resampling - for example a bootstrap with replacement or a jackknife. The distribution on the fitted slope would determine the robustness of the fitted parameter.
Section 4.3 describes one of the more important steps of the analysis, that is the definition of the GC subpopulations. After a lengthy description of the method and of the parameters, the method is finally applied to the data and results in 4 “reliable” components. But then the application of the method on a *different* sample (the old GCs) returns 3 components and this somehow motivates 3 components for the whole sample. The actual statistical tests that have been made and that quantify the robustness of the different components is barely mentioned in the main text and is completely hidden in the appendix. I would clearly state the tests done in the main text and their conclusions. Only after this comes the comparison with the old population and the possible interpretation of the different metallicity distributions. A final plot like figure H1 and H2 needs to be shown in the main text for the final definition of the three components along with the corresponding numbers. The conclusions of this paper hinge on this step and cannot be buried in the appendix.
Something I would try to explore is whether the distribution of metallicities changes with radius. If the number of GCs allows it, I suggest to divide the sample in two, or even three radial bins, and see how the distributions evolve. Are three subpopulation visible at all radii? Are the gradients traced by a shift in the mean metallicities of the MP and MR populations or are the distributions different?
The comparison with TNG50 is unsatisfactory. An analog of NGC5128 is found based on integrated properties and merger history. By observing that i) the simulated red GC population extends in the outer halo of the host as the MR population of NGC5128 and that ii) both galaxy had mergers, the conclusion is that mergers are responsible for this property. This is not demonstrated in this comparison and, while “”such a configuration is naturally expected in systems that have experienced a complex merger history” (line 1260), at this point there should be at least some references of work in the literature that actually checked this. The point of using simulations would be either i) to trace the simulated galaxy back in time and actually study how the merger redistributes the GCs with radius or ii) take a sample of galaxies with and without important mergers and compare their distribution of GCs. This is probably beyond the scope of the paper but could be the starting point for a follow up publication. As it is, I suggest to remove this part.
The intermediate-age GCs are interpreted as formed in the satellite that merged with NGC5128. Are the ages consistent with those of the intermediate-age halo stars? If these GCs actually formed in the satellite as triggered by the merger, as suggested in Sect. 5.1, are these ages consistent with the epoch of the merger that created the dust lane, or is this something else?
The intermediate metallicity, old population is also interpreted to be accreted based on the flat metallicity gradient. I am wondering about the interpretation of this component. Is this potentially coming from the same satellite that merged with Cen A together with the intermediate-age population? A comparison with the mass-metallicity relation should return a potential mass for the accreted satellite, perhaps this can be useful to put this component into context.
I have the impression that after a lot of work to obtain metallicities and separate populations, the paper does not fully capitalise on the results. In my opinion the next step would be to study the spatial distribution of the three population: are the density profiles systematically different? If the intermediate metallicity population is actually accreted, I would expect a more shallow distribution with radius compared to the MR. This would strengthen the conclusions derived from the metallicity gradients.  It would also be interesting to see if the different populations are somewhat clustered in space, e.g. azimuthally segregated, or smoothly distributed. You could try to show 2D maps for the three metallicity components. There is a section 5.3 that connects the intermediate population with the streams, but the whole analysis is hidden in appendix M. Please consider moving this interesting part to the main text. Table M1 is also very interesting. Perhaps it would be useful to compare the properties of the GCs in the streams with those of the GC in the relaxed halo.
